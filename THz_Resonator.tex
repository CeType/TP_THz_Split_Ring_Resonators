\begin{abstract}
Photonic metamaterials, artificial crystals engineered by nano- and microfabrication techniques, are a well-studied platform to achieve optical properties beyond those observed in nature.
In particular, it is known in theory and experiment that arrays of split-ring resonators can unlock magnetic effects in optics, allowing to observe a negative refractive index\cite{lindenPhotonicMetamaterialsMagnetism2006a}.
By working in the THz frequency range, it becomes feasible to fabricate such metamaterials by UV lithography and observe the narrow absorbtion peak predicted by theory.
Here, we replicate these known results on two different geometries of split-ring resonator (SRR) based metamaterials.
\end{abstract}
